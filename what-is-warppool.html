What is Warppool?

A website you visited uses Warppool to earn revenue without ads. Instead of showing you banners and tracking your browsing, it asks to borrow a tiny sliver of your device's GPU to run useful scientific computation. You always choose whether to opt in.

How it works

Three steps. Nothing hidden.

You see a prompt

When a site uses Warppool, a small pop-up asks for your permission. Nothing runs until you say yes. You can choose "Just this visit," "Always allow," or "No thanks."

Your GPU does small tasks

If you opt in, your browser's GPU runs small chunks of a researcher's computation — things like climate simulations, statistics, or machine learning. Each chunk finishes in a few seconds.

The site earns revenue

The website earns a small payment for each completed task, funded by the researchers who submitted the job. When you close the tab, everything stops immediately.

Your protections

What Warppool guarantees to every visitor.

  • Always opt-in. Nothing ever runs without your explicit consent. No background mining, no hidden scripts.
  • No personal data. Warppool never reads your cookies, browsing history, location, or any personal information. The GPU processes numbers, not your data.
  • No tracking. There are no ad networks, no retargeting pixels, and no data brokers involved. Your browsing remains private.
  • Sandboxed by your browser. Compute runs inside your browser's own GPU sandbox. It cannot access your files, camera, microphone, or anything outside the tab.
  • Minimal resource use. A Warppool worker adds roughly 1–2 watts to your device — less than your screen backlight. It runs at idle priority and pauses if your GPU is busy.
  • Stops when you leave. Close the tab, navigate away, or revoke permission — computation stops instantly. There is no background process left behind.
Common concern

Is this crypto mining?

No. Warppool is fundamentally different from cryptocurrency mining. Every cycle your GPU contributes does real, useful computation — climate modeling, statistical analysis, machine learning research. Nothing is wasted on hash puzzles or proof-of-work.

Cryptominers typically run without consent inside ads, drain your battery, and benefit only the site operator. Warppool requires explicit opt-in, shows you exactly what's running, throttles to background priority, and funds open research.

Verification built in. Every task is checked by 2–3 independent workers. Canary tasks detect bad actors. You can see the job name and submitter in the consent prompt before you agree to anything.
Under the hood

Technical details for the curious.

Browser-native GPU compute

Warppool taps your browser's built-in GPU compute — the same capability that powers web-based games and 3D applications. It runs compute shaders (small GPU programs) in a secure, sandboxed environment controlled by your browser.

Task distribution

A coordinator server splits large jobs into tiny chunks (a few seconds of work each) and sends them to available workers over a WebSocket connection. Your browser runs one chunk at a time and sends back the result.

What kind of work?

Current workloads include Monte Carlo simulations, climate ensemble forecasts, hyperparameter sweeps for machine learning, and batched inference on open models. All tasks are embarrassingly parallel — meaning each chunk is independent.

Open source

The Warppool coordinator and worker client are open source. You can inspect exactly what code runs in your browser. The compute shaders (written in WGSL) are fetched from the server and are visible in your browser's developer tools.

Your choices

You are always in control.

What happens if I click "Always allow"?

Your browser remembers your choice for that site. The next time you visit, the worker starts automatically without showing the prompt. You can revoke this at any time by clearing site data in your browser settings.

What happens if I click "Just this visit"?

The worker runs for this session only. When you close the tab, permission is forgotten. The next time you visit, you'll see the prompt again.

What happens if I click "No thanks"?

Nothing runs. The widget closes or minimizes. You won't be asked again during this visit. The website continues to work normally — Warppool never blocks content or degrades your experience for declining.

Can I stop it after opting in?

Yes. Close the tab, navigate away, or click the minimize/stop button on the widget. Computation stops immediately. If you previously chose "Always allow," clear site data for that domain to reset your preference.

Does this affect my battery or performance?

Minimally. A Warppool worker adds about 1–2 watts of power draw — comparable to having an extra browser tab open. It runs at idle priority, meaning your own work always comes first. If your GPU is busy with a game or video, Warppool waits.
